# Conceptual Tension Linework

**ID:** `conceptual-tension-linework`

## Core Direction

Reduce the subject and its narrative relationships into an extremely restrained conceptual system of thin black hand-drawn lines.

The image should communicate tension, restriction, pressure, crossing, resistance, transition, and release without directly illustrating every detail.

## Transformation

Do not trace the photograph.

Extract:

- direction;
- connection;
- contradiction;
- pressure;
- turning points;
- essential symbolic silhouette.

Translate these relationships into continuous or near-continuous black lines.

Lines may:

- enter from the edge;
- bend;
- cross;
- tighten;
- loop;
- reverse;
- rise;
- pull outward;
- partially reveal the subject's recognizable form.

The silhouette may remain incomplete.

## Line Behavior

Use thin, restrained, slightly imperfect ink-like lines.

Allow subtle variation in line weight and pressure.

Small structural marks such as:

- nodes;
- barbs;
- short transverse marks;
- interruptions

should appear only where tension, intersection, or directional change occurs.

## Composition

Use a cold white, off-white, or very low-saturation pale background.

Favor large negative space.

Keep the main line system:

- off-center;
- lower in the frame;
- near an edge;
- relatively small.

Avoid filling the canvas.

## Color

Primarily monochrome.

If color is used, keep it extremely limited and subordinate to the line system.

## Typography

Default:

```text
TEXT_MODE = micro
```

Optional content:

- one short thematic word;
- a number;
- a place;
- a restrained phrase.

Use thin, quiet typography that behaves like a small trace within the line system.

It must not become the visual center.

## Mood

```text
quiet
tense
conceptual
restrained
serious
poetic
unresolved
```

## Avoid

- decorative curves without meaning;
- colorful fills;
- realistic shading;
- complex scenery;
- 3D rendering;
- cartoon styling;
- commercial advertising aesthetics;
- decorative visual abundance.
