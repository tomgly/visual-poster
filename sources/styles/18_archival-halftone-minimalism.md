# Archival Halftone Minimalism

**ID:** `archival-halftone-minimalism`

## Core Direction

Reduce the source into one highly recognizable visual anchor using extreme negative space, limited ink colors, silkscreen texture, halftone transitions, and restrained editorial typography.

The composition should feel like a collectible art print, architectural journal, or independent exhibition poster.

## Transformation

Compress complex information into:

```text
one subject
one direction
one spatial relationship
```

Retain only the single most important:

- shape;
- gesture;
- movement;
- boundary;
- spatial relation.

Use the minimum amount of information required for immediate recognition.

## Composition

Keep the subject relatively small.

Use large negative space to create:

- distance;
- silence;
- scale;
- poetry.

Choose one dominant structural behavior based on the source, such as:

- horizontal extension;
- vertical extension;
- isolated suspension;
- outward dispersion;
- shallow-to-deep recession.

Do not combine multiple composition devices unnecessarily.

Use a single horizon, boundary, shadow, or halftone band when needed to establish space.

## Color

Reorganize the source atmosphere into:

```text
paper base
+
main ink
+
mist layer
```

Use approximately 2–3 colors.

Possible paper bases include:

- off-white;
- warm gray;
- natural paper.

Use one quiet, substantial main ink color.

Use a lighter or grayer related tone for:

- air;
- distance;
- shadow;
- spatial transition.

Use a tiny accent color only when it carries clear narrative meaning.

## Halftone

Represent mist, distance, fading, shadow, dispersion, and transitions through changing halftone density.

Halftone dots should gradually become sparse and disappear naturally.

Do not use conventional digital blur or smooth digital gradients.

## Materials

Preserve:

- paper grain;
- silkscreen ink;
- uneven ink coverage;
- subtle overprint misregistration;
- authentic printed-art texture.

## Typography

Default:

```text
TEXT_MODE = editorial
```

Use:

- one extremely short main theme;
- one to three groups of very small supporting text.

Content may derive from:

- theme;
- emotion;
- time;
- motion;
- material;
- distance;
- metaphor.

Possible supporting elements include:

- microtext;
- year;
- number;
- chapter number;
- coordinate-like figures;
- archive marks;
- short literary phrases.

Typography should actively participate in the composition.

It may:

- align with a horizon;
- follow a silhouette;
- form a vertical axis;
- cross a halftone boundary;
- hide inside negative space;
- interact through masking, interruption, insertion, or alignment.

Use restrained sans-serif, quiet serif, or very thin minimal type.

## Mood

```text
quiet
solitary
expansive
poetic
intellectual
archival
curatorial
collectible
```

## Avoid

- multiple competing focal points;
- complex composition;
- meaningless decoration;
- oversized titles;
- commercial advertising copy;
- conventional gradients;
- cheap retro filters;
- template aesthetics.
