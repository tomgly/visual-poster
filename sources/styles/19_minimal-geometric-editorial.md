# Minimal Geometric Editorial

**ID:** `minimal-geometric-editorial`

## Core Direction

Transform the source subject into a high-end minimal editorial poster language built from recognizable structure, concise geometry, flat color fields, thin linework, and generous negative space.

The result should feel contemporary, quiet, refined, and art-directed, drawing from international design studios, architectural posters, art-exhibition graphics, and luxury visual identity systems.

The source must remain immediately recognizable even after abstraction.

## Transformation

Extract the subject's most distinctive silhouette, structural relationships, proportions, and directional features.

Reduce them into:

```text
recognizable contour
+
concise geometric shapes
+
flat color fields
+
thin structural lines
+
negative space
```

Favor abstraction over illustration.

Use the minimum visual information required to preserve identity.

Avoid realistic redrawing, literal tracing, ornamental detail, or unnecessary complexity.

Small horizontal lines, vertical lines, framing marks, or abstract environmental cues may be added when they clarify space or structure.

## Composition

Use a restrained, balanced composition with the subject centered or optically centered.

Preserve generous breathing room around the subject.

Favor:

- large areas of negative space;
- precise visual hierarchy;
- calm asymmetry or controlled central alignment;
- architectural spacing;
- deliberate relationships between image, line, and typography.

Do not overcrowd the canvas.

When used with `compare-split`, apply this transformation to the lower section while allowing the output-mode rules to control the exact 50/50 split and source-photo treatment.

## Color

Default:

```text
COLOR_MODE = source-derived
```

Extract a restrained palette from the source image.

Favor:

- beige;
- warm off-white;
- pale neutral backgrounds;
- muted source-derived colors;
- low-to-medium saturation;
- one or two stronger structural accents only when needed.

Color should feel curated rather than decorative.

Maintain a clear relationship between the source atmosphere and the abstracted result.

## Linework and Geometry

Use thin, precise linework and simple geometric construction.

Suitable forms include:

- rectangles;
- circles;
- arcs;
- planes;
- cropped silhouettes;
- simplified architectural boundaries;
- sparse horizontal or vertical guide lines.

Geometry should describe the subject rather than become an unrelated pattern system.

Avoid excessive vector polish or sterile technical-diagram aesthetics.

## Materials / Rendering

Keep surfaces primarily flat and clean, with subtle print or paper character when useful.

Suitable qualities include:

- matte paper;
- soft printed color;
- restrained grain;
- subtle tonal variation;
- refined editorial finish.

Do not introduce glossy 3D materials, synthetic lighting effects, metallic rendering, neon glow, or overtly electronic visual language.

## Typography

Default:

```text
TEXT_MODE = minimal
```

Use only a small amount of concise English typography.

Suitable elements include:

- a short title;
- a year;
- a style or category label;
- one small secondary line.

Typography should be sparse, controlled, and integrated into the composition rather than used as advertising copy.

Favor refined sans-serif or restrained modern serif typography with generous spacing.

```text
quiet

```

## Avoid

- photorealistic illustration in the transformed section;
- dense descriptive detail;
- multiple competing focal points;
- unnecessary decoration;
- cartoon styling;
- cute character treatment;
- generic reflections or glossy effects;
- cheap low-quality rendering;
- overtly digital or electronic aesthetics;
- neon or cyber styling;
- stock-template composition;
- commercial advertising clutter;
- stretched or distorted subject geometry;
- abstraction that makes the source unrecognizable.
